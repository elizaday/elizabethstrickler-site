The five shifts

A script for a shift still in progress

Five signs I use to read what AI is doing to creative work. Each has two readings that pull against each other, and neither one cancels the other. Most organizations are living inside that pull, which is where the friction, the fear and the opportunity sit.

Curation

in tension withCraft

Technical skill is getting cheap and taste is getting scarce, yet taste is trained by making. The ability to choose rises in value, and the eye that chooses well still comes from the hands.

Shows up astaste, judgment, selection, omission

What to doBuild a practice that sharpens your taste and makes it visible. Lean into your weird. Get known for it.

Influence

in tension withIP

Value used to sit in owning a finished asset. More and more of it sits in circulation: who carries an idea, who remixes it, and who keeps coming back.

Shows up asnetworks, publics, cultural gravity

What to doProtect the core. Design the rest to travel. Decide whether you are building a fortress or a movement.

Leverage

in tension withLabor

Large, role-based crews give way to small teams with outsized reach. When the machine can supply the hours, the scarce input becomes direction.

Shows up assmall crews, scale, the one-person studio

What to doUse the machine to scale a vision you already have. It will not supply one.

Worldbuilding

in tension withWorkflow

Script, shoot, edit is a line. A world is a system you return to: characters, lore and assets that hold together across formats and keep producing stories.

Shows up aspersistence, lore, reusable universes

What to doStop making one-offs. Invest in what can carry many stories.

Ecosystem

in tension withAudience

The audience stops sitting in the dark. It talks back, makes things, and expects to be let in.

Shows up asparticipation, community, feedback loops

What to doDesign the loop. Build with the people who show up.

The core idea

The product is not proof.

When a machine can produce competent output on demand, the finished thing stops telling you who understood anything. My work circles one problem: what still counts as evidence of learning, of expertise, of authorship and of intention, and how to teach people to produce it.

Open questions

Signs I have not finished reading

The questions underneath the work. I do not have the answers yet, and I am suspicious of anyone who claims to.

What becomes scarce when competent production is nearly free?

What happens to expertise when machines can imitate everything it visibly produces?

What happens to attention under infinite generation?

Who owns the systems, who gets paid, and where does the leverage pile up?

What do authorship and intention mean once human and machine agency are tangled together?

Who am I talking to inside my own head, and does that change when the machine talks back?
